NEW MEXICO AND THE WAR ECONOMY

LEARN MORE

New Mexico contains a whole network of war infrastructure: three air force bases, two national labs, one massive weapons testing site, and a web of private contractors that feed off these facilities. Many look at this ‘defense’ industry as a neutral economic actor that helps bring federal dollars to our state. However, it is an enemy to our land, our people, and our fight for dignified lives. From poisoning our land, water, and people to providing the pilots, machines, bombs, and missiles that destroy cities and lives the world over, this war industry stands in the way of a peaceful, bountiful future.

DISCUSS

Questions

  1. People often make the argument that the ‘defense industry’ (i.e., the war industry) is good for New Mexico’s economy. What do you think about this argument? How would you respond to people with this perspective?
  2. How do we manage the contradiction between the harm the war economy causes in New Mexico and/or the world and the ways in which our economy is premised on its success? How do we grapple with the reality that many workers are forced and/or incentivized to work within it?
  3. Where do you see the war economy’s presence in your life? What threads of your life (e.g. work, school, resources, etc.) are entangled in this industry?
  4. Every new economic system carries over some pieces of the structures that preceded it. What seeds of a peace economy already exist in New Mexico and your community that you feel excited about? What are we already creating that can carry over into a new system?

About Workers for a New Southwest (WNS)

“WNS exists to build a fighting socialist movement in New Mexico. We take New Mexico’s real conditions—scarce water, rising rents, land loss, and a war and extraction economy—and treat them as the ground for new working-class institutions. We aim to link labor fights, tenant work, and community defense into one front. We reject the idea that workers need either duopoly party, nonprofits, or wealthy patrons to act. We push people to move as a class with their own institutions, strategy, and discipline.”

– from the pamphlet “About WNS”
